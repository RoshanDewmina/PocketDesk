# PocketDesk Competitive Landscape — Sep 2026

Research date 28 Sep 2026, ~7 weeks before target launch (17 Nov 2026). Vendor claims are marked **[V]**; independent/user evidence **[I]**. Where I could not find hard data I say so rather than guess.

## Per-product findings

**Jump Desktop (iOS) + Jump Desktop Connect (Fluid protocol)**
Jump Desktop 10 shipped 24 Aug 2026, with point releases through late Aug fixing multi-monitor pointer bugs, CJK typing, and NVIDIA color issues [[changelog]](https://changelog.jumpdesktop.com/). Fluid 2.0 **[V]** claims ~50% better efficiency than Fluid 1, adds H.265/HEVC and 4:4:4 chroma, up to six 4K host displays or four 4K virtual displays, 60fps, and real-time audio [[Fluid 2.0 notes]](https://changelog.jumpdesktop.com/jump-desktop-with-fluid-2.0-1yMoBG). A 2020 vendor tweet claims "60fps with ~10ms audio latency on a good connection" **[V, old, unverified for 2026 build]** [[X post]](https://x.com/jumpdesktop/status/1251483953913106435); no independent frame-timing benchmark was found. Pricing: one-time $29.99 Mac / $14.99 iOS, plus Jump Desktop Connect at $4/computer/month for relay-based zero-config pairing [[Mustafa.net]](https://mustafa.net/2026/03/12/jump-desktop-the-best-remote-desktop-app-for-ipad-mac-pc/). App Store ~4.5★. Complaints **[I]**: Fluid doesn't always hit full Retina res, features increasingly gated behind the subscription for people who already paid, no E2E encryption [[SeaSalt]](https://seasalt.app/blog/jump-desktop-alternative-mac), plus a two-finger-scroll speed mismatch and a Caps-Lock/stuck-Cmd-key bug reported in support threads [[Jump Desktop Support]](https://support.jumpdesktop.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/360000013903-Need-ability-to-adjust-scroll-wheel-trackpad-scroll-sensitivity). Interaction model: trackpad-style with a customizable on-screen keyboard/shortcut toolbar.

**Screens 5 (Edovia)**
Current build 5.8.5 (15 Apr 2026) with further updates through 30 Jul and 15 Sep 2026 [[release notes]](https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/faq/release-notes). As of 20 Jun 2026, remote access is built directly into Screens — no separate Screens Connect install [[Edovia]](https://edovia.com/en/screens). Pricing: $2.99/mo, $24.99/yr, or $99 lifetime IAP [[Edovia pricing]](https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/faq/s5-pricing). Notable feature: an IAP that turns a second iPhone into a trackpad while an iPad runs the session [[justuseapp]](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/655890150/screens/reviews). App Store ~4.6★. Complaints **[I]**: Screens 4 owners angry that Screens 5 is a paid subscription relaunch rather than a free update, and no genuine free trial (must pay for month one) [[justuseapp]](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1663047912/screens-5-vnc-remote-desktop/reviews).

**RustDesk (iOS client / macOS host)**
Stable 1.4.6 (5 Mar 2026) [[Wikipedia]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RustDesk). Fully open source; free tier is self-hosted/perpetual with unlimited devices; commercial Server Pro from ~$19.90/mo for 10 users [[RustDesk pricing]](https://rustdesk.com/pricing/). macOS host needs Screen Recording + Accessibility permissions granted manually [[digi-tools]](https://digi-tools.info/articles/rustdesk-review). iOS client gives on-screen touchpad, mouse mode, software keyboard, file transfer [[RustDesk blog]](https://rustdesk.com/blog/rustdesk-remote-control-android-ios/). App Store ~4.1★. Complaints **[I]**: key-mismatch errors on iPad, window-vanishes-but-stays-active bug, no Apple Pencil support, and public relay congestion — reviewers explicitly recommend self-hosting for usable performance [[justuseapp]](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1581225015/rustdesk-remote-desktop/reviews).

**Astropad Workbench** — the strongest recent native entrant. Launched publicly 8 Apr 2026 with coverage from MacRumors, 9to5Mac, AppleInsider, TechCrunch and MacStories, positioned as "remote desktop made for the AI era" for headless Mac minis and AI coding agents [[MacRumors]](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/08/astropad-workbench-app/) [[TechCrunch]](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/08/astropads-workbench-reimagines-remote-desktop-for-ai-agents-not-it-support/). From the Luna Display/Astropad team, so design pedigree is high. Uses "Liquid" streaming **[V]**: perceptually-lossless Retina, adaptive codec, combines multiple Mac displays into one screen scaled to the client's resolution; supports Apple Pencil, touch gestures, keyboard, and voice dictation of prompts [[MacStories]](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/astropad-workbench-rethinks-remote-mac-control-for-ai-agents/). Free tier = 20–30 min/day; unlimited is $10/mo or $50/yr [[AppleInsider]](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/04/08/remote-desktop-for-mac-is-shifting-toward-ai-workloads-with-workbench). Early App Store reviews praise resolution scaling across devices [[automateed]](https://www.automateed.com/astropad-workbench-review); no independent latency numbers found yet.

**Remote Mac Desktop Control** — closest interaction-model match to PocketDesk. Free with IAP, App Store ID 6790186904 [[App Store]](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/remote-mac-desktop-control/id6790186904). Trackpad-first: tap/scroll/drag gestures, full keyboard, live low-latency screen mirror, a "VR mode" that wraps the desktop around the viewer. QR-code pairing with certificate pinning, cryptographically signed commands, no account required, open-source notarized Mac helper. No independent review volume found yet — worth hands-on testing since its stated model overlaps PocketDesk's almost exactly.

**Moonlight (iOS) / Sunshine (macOS host) / Apollo+Artemis** — Moonlight iOS remains an actively maintained open-source GameStream client; Sunshine ships official macOS builds (last updated 26 May 2026) alongside Windows/Linux [[LizardByte]](https://app.lizardbyte.dev/Sunshine/). Apollo (Sunshine fork with a virtual display auto-sized to the connecting client) and Artemis (its Moonlight-derived client) are documented for Windows; no confirmed macOS support for Apollo's virtual-display feature was found [[tech-insider]](https://tech-insider.org/apollo-vs-sunshine-game-streaming-2026/). This stack is gaming/GPU-encode-oriented and macOS hardware encode quality here is unverified — treat as a lower-priority, free-to-test baseline rather than a direct threat.

**Parsec** — confirmed **no iOS/iPad app and no iOS web client**; official support docs list only Windows/Linux/macOS/Android as controllable/joinable platforms [[Parsec support]](https://support.parsec.app/hc/en-us/articles/32381568346644-Hardware-and-Software-Compatibility). Exclude from the iPhone comparison.

**WhipDesk** — turns out to be a developer tool for monitoring/controlling AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor) from a mobile browser, not a general desktop-streaming app: no app install, WebRTC/DTLS peer-to-peer on LAN, optional account for remote relay [[WhipDesk]](https://whipdesk.com/). Adjacent, not a direct PocketDesk competitor.

**Offsite** — I could not find any remote-desktop or agent-control product by this name; the only "Offsite" with any web presence is an unrelated corporate-retreat-planning SaaS (offsite.com). Likely either very obscure/pre-launch, or a name mix-up on the brief — flag back to confirm rather than report on it further.

**Apple native options** — "High Performance" Screen Sharing (macOS, Mac↔Mac only, no iPhone client) requires both Macs on Apple Silicon + macOS 14+, ~75 Mbps for one 4K stream, UDP 5900–5902 open, and delivers 4:4:4 chroma, HDR reference mode, stereo audio, 30/60fps, up to a 4K virtual display [[Apple/9to5Mac]](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/05/how-to-use-macos-high-performance-screen-sharing-for-lower-latency-and-better-color-video/). macOS 27 adds **direct touch** to Sidecar (previously mouse/Pencil-only) [[MacRumors]](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/09/macos-27-brings-direct-touch-control-to-sidecar/) — but Sidecar is iPad-as-second-screen, not iPhone remote control, and a touchscreen MacBook is rumored for late 2026. iPhone Mirroring runs the opposite direction (Mac controls iPhone) and just gained Live Activities in the Mac menu bar [[9to5Mac]](https://9to5mac.com/2025/09/23/iphone-mirroring-on-the-mac-gets-a-key-upgrade-in-macos-tahoe/). **Bottom line: Apple still ships no first-party iPhone→Mac remote-control client** — that gap is the whole market PocketDesk and the above apps compete in.

## 2. Where PocketDesk stands

- **Behind:** actual measured frame delivery (15–28fps vs Jump's/Apple's 60fps claims); no HEVC/4:4:4 option (competitors increasingly tout this for text clarity); no owned relay/TURN yet, so remote access outside Tailscale/LAN is behind Jump Desktop Connect, Screens' built-in remote access, RustDesk's public relay, and Workbench's cloud relay — all of which "just work" with no user networking config.
- **At parity:** QR pairing + host-side approval flow (matches RustDesk/Remote Mac Desktop Control patterns); free local-network access (matches RustDesk, Workbench's capped free tier).
- **Ahead:** interaction polish — dock gesture, click haptics, edge auto-pan, and a dedicated pinch-zoom/Fill-Fit model are more thought-through than most competitors, which mostly ship a plain relative trackpad with no haptic or auto-pan feedback.

**Top 5 gaps to close before 17 Nov 2026:**
1. Fix the frame-delivery bug — this alone undercuts every performance claim on the product page.
2. Add HEVC (and ideally a 4:4:4 "Sharper" mode) — it's now a checkbox item competitors lead with.
3. Ship the own relay/TURN service (or at least a documented interim, e.g. hosted Tailscale-lite) before charging for "remote access" — Workbench and RustDesk both give meaningful free remote access, raising the bar for what a paid tier must justify.
4. Add an on-screen customizable keyboard/shortcut toolbar and optional audio streaming — both are called out positively for Jump Desktop and are plausible expectations at launch.
5. Get independent, repeatable latency/fps numbers before launch messaging goes out — no competitor has published rigorous third-party benchmarks either, so being first with credible data is a differentiator, not just risk mitigation.

## 3. Head-to-head test protocol (iPhone 17 + MacBook Air M4, same Wi-Fi)

**Test order (cheapest first):** (1) RustDesk — free, self-host the relay for a fair LAN test; (2) Remote Mac Desktop Control — free, closest interaction model to PocketDesk; (3) Astropad Workbench — free 20–30 min/day tier is enough per run; (4) Jump Desktop — check for a trial, otherwise budget ~$45 one-time; (5) Screens 5 — no free trial found, budget $2.99 for one month, test last.

**Tasks, per app:**
- **Latency (click-to-photon):** Load a millisecond-precision flashing/counter HTML page full-screen on the Mac. Point a *second* camera (another phone or the iPhone 17 held by a stand while a second device streams) at 240fps slow-mo, framing both the Mac's physical screen and the test iPhone's screen simultaneously. Tap a button in the remote app that toggles the timer's color; count frames between the physical tap and the color change appearing on the Mac, and separately between the Mac's change and it appearing back on the iPhone screen. This isolates input latency from round-trip display latency.
- **Update rate / smoothness:** Use the iPhone's native 60fps screen recording (Control Center) to record the app's own display while performing a fixed, repeatable action on the Mac — a scripted mouse sweep across the screen or a scrolling long text/code document at constant speed. Run each recording through `ffmpeg -i rec.mov -vf "mpdecimate=hi=64*12:lo=64*5:frac=0.33,metadata=mode=print" -f null -` to flag duplicate frames, then compute distinct-frame timestamps to get instantaneous fps, median gap, and p90 gap — the same metric PocketDesk already uses internally (33ms median / 117ms p90 baseline).
- **Small-text readability:** Display a fixed-size monospace code sample or standard reading-acuity chart on the Mac; screenshot via the iPhone at both Fill and Fit view; compare edge/chroma artifacts around glyphs at 100% zoom crops (this exposes H.264 4:2:0 vs HEVC 4:4:4 differences).
- **Pointer tracking:** Trace a fixed shape (e.g. a printed circle template on the Mac screen) using the relative trackpad at a controlled speed; record and overlay traces to compare overshoot/lag between apps.

**What to compare across apps:** distinct fps and gap percentiles from mpdecimate, click-to-photon frame counts (ms) from the 240fps footage, subjective text-clarity score at 1:1 zoom, and pointer-trace deviation — plus setup time (stopwatch from "tap app icon" to "control established") and whether remote (off-LAN) access required any manual network configuration.
