Rebuilding Controls

The dock stays exactly as it is. This page is about the sheet behind its sliders button: what it holds today, three ways to rebuild it, and which one to pick.

Frames are an iPhone 17 in a live session (402 × 874 pt), drawn in Reach: void background, bone type, SF Pro, ember only where something touches the Mac. Nothing here is built yet.

What the sheet holds today

Controls opens at half height and scrolls through eight groups on main. The phone-parity branch adds four more (Display, Touch, Mini map, Keyboard and pointer), so after that merge it is twelve. The first screen shows Fill/Fit, a zoom slider and half of the pointer buttons. Mission Control and the Space keys sit below the fold: fifth group on main, eighth after the merge.

Three groups repeat the dock: Fill/Fit, View/Control and the clipboard actions are all one tap away there already. While the sheet is open the trackpad is switched off, so you can't steer and look at the result at the same time.

A

Mac keys, settings one tap deeper Recommended

The sheet becomes a short, fixed-height panel of keys for things you do to the Mac. Each key prints its Mac shortcut, so the phone teaches the keyboard as you use it. Settings you change once a month move to a native grouped page pushed inside the same sheet.

  • Everything used mid-session is in two rows the thumb reaches without shifting grip. Nothing scrolls.
  • The panel stops at about 40% of the screen and does not dim the Mac. The trackpad keeps working above it, so you can open Mission Control, then point at a window and click it with the panel still open.
  • Space keys keep the panel open, so three Space switches are three taps. Swipe down or tap Done to close.
  • Show Desktop is a new key. It sends F11, which the host can post once the parity branch's F-key map is merged.

Costs: settings are one tap further away, and a stray touch on the picture above the panel now reaches the Mac, the same as when the panel is closed. Landscape needs its own one-row panel.

B

A tray on top of the dock

No sheet. The sliders button in the dock raises a tray of the same keys directly above the dock panel, so the actions sit where the thumb already is. Settings open as a full page from the tray.

  • No change of context: the dock stays on screen and the keys are 60 pt from its buttons.
  • Fastest path for someone who already has the dock open.

Costs: dock plus tray cover the lower 55% of the screen and the dot screen darkens the rest, so the Mac is hardest to see here. The open dock also looks different, which works against keeping the dock as it is. It is a custom container rather than a system sheet.

C

The same sheet, tidied

Keep a half-height native sheet, but put eight small keys at the top and fold every other group into a one-line summary row that pushes its own page: Picture · Sharper, Pointer · Medium, Touch · Trackpad. Twelve groups become one grid and about eight rows.

  • Smallest change to the code and the most familiar pattern: it reads like the Settings app.
  • Every setting's current value is visible without opening it.

Costs: it is still a modal sheet that turns the trackpad off and dims the Mac. The keys sit at the top of the sheet, the farthest point from the thumb, and the list still scrolls at half height.

Recommendation: A

A is the only option that keeps the Mac visible and steerable while the actions are open, and it puts the keys in the thumb's reach instead of at the top of a sheet. It also removes the three groups that duplicate the dock, which is most of why today's sheet feels long.

Take one idea from C: the Settings page in A should use C's summary rows, so each setting shows its current value before you open it.

What A changes in code, when approved: the sheet gets one fixed height with background interaction enabled (SwiftUI's presentationBackgroundInteraction, iOS 16.4+); the trackpad stays enabled while it is open; Settings becomes a pushed page in the sheet's existing navigation stack; landscape gets a one-row panel. The dock is not touched.

Drag, with words that say what is happening

After a double-tap-and-hold drag, a bone button reading “Release” with a raised hand appears over the dock handle, and the dock status says “Holding · tap Release to drop”. A raised hand reads as “stop”, and “Release” doesn't say what is being released or that lifting your finger already does it.

There are two different holds, and they need different words:

  • Finger drag (double-tap, hold, slide). Lifting drops the item, so there is nothing to press. Show a status line, “Holding click · lift to drop”, with the ember contact dot. No button.
  • Hold click from Controls. The button stays down after you lift, so the chip carries the action: “Mouse button held”, “Slide one finger to move it. Drops by itself in 8 s”, and a Drop button. The dock tile that replaces Mic during a hold says Drop too.
