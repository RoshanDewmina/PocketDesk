farside24 studies · iPhone, iPad and Mac · 28 Sep 2026

Farside motion lab · app

Twenty-four small motions for the app and the Mac menu bar. Each one answers a question people are already asking: did it connect, did it click, is it still there. Drag on the black stages, scroll on the text, and press ↻ to replay. Pick the ones worth building by number.

  • Ember only for contact
  • Text sits on solid plates, never on dots
  • The haptic chip lights when the phone would tap you
  • Pauses off-screen · every card has a Reduce Motion still

No. 01Connect

Closing the gap

Tap Connect and the distance to your Mac counts down to zero. The ember dot where they meet is the proof.

Where it goes
Home to live session, the moment the connection is up. The same art waits on Home before you connect.
Build it in SwiftUI
KeyframeAnimator drives gap and glow; TimelineView(.animation(minimumInterval: 1/30, paused: !visible)) with a Canvas draws the halftone; the readout uses .contentTransition(.numericText(countsDown: true)).
Haptic
.success at contact, together with the ember ripple.

No. 02Connect

The portal

The ember arrow on Connect opens into your Mac. You go in through the button you pressed.

Where it goes
Home to live session. A quicker alternative to No. 01 for people who connect ten times a day.
Build it in SwiftUI
iOS 18 .matchedTransitionSource(id: "connect", in: ns) on the arrow and .navigationTransition(.zoom(sourceID: "connect", in: ns)) on the session, or a Circle() mask scaled with .timingCurve(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1, duration: 0.7).
Haptic
.impact(weight: .medium) on press, .success when the first frame lands.

No. 03Connect

Scouting party

Dots try the straight road first. If the café router says no, they take the relay. You always see which road won.

Where it goes
The connecting screen, and the route label in the dock footer (Direct or Relayed).
Build it in SwiftUI
One Canvas in a TimelineView; the winning route draws with Path.trimmedPath(from: 0, to: progress); latency text uses .contentTransition(.numericText()).
Haptic
None while scouting. .success when a route locks.

No. 04Connect

Resolution lock

Your Mac arrives as noise and sharpens in four ticks: Bayer 2×2, 4×4, 8×8, then the real picture.

Where it goes
First frames of a session. The dots are a stand-in built from the first keyframe; the live stream itself is never dithered. Steps follow real connection events, so it never adds a wait.
Build it in SwiftUI
.layerEffect(ShaderLibrary.bayerLock(.float(cell), .float(order)), maxSampleOffset: .zero) on the stand-in, stepped by .phaseAnimator([.noise, .b2, .b4, .b8, .crisp], trigger: step).
Haptic
Three rigid ticks with rising sharpness (CHHapticPattern), then .success on crisp.

No. 05Connect

Pairing lands in the mark

Once your Mac approves, the code dissolves and flies into the mark. Paired means the dots moved in.

Where it goes
Pairing, after the scan and the approval on the Mac. The code expires in two minutes, like a mayfly with a job.
Build it in SwiftUI
One Canvas and a precomputed module-to-mark map, driven by KeyframeAnimator; the burst is a .phaseAnimator ring. Don’t build 441 views.
Haptic
.success at the burst.

No. 06Pointer

Settle halo

When the pointer stops, a ring of dots says “here”. Then it leaves quietly.

Where it goes
Live session, after any move longer than a few points. Same size at every zoom level.
Build it in SwiftUI
.phaseAnimator([.hidden, .bloom, .fade], trigger: settleCount); “settled” is a 120 ms debounce with Task.sleep(for: .milliseconds(120)).
Haptic
None. Stopping is not an event.

No. 07Pointer

Speed trail

Fling the pointer and it drops a short trail of dots so your eyes can catch up. Slow moves leave nothing.

Where it goes
Live session, only above roughly 1,800 pt/s.
Build it in SwiftUI
TimelineView(.animation) plus Canvas over a ring buffer of the last 12 samples; Canvas(renderer:symbols:) resolves one dot once and stamps it.
Haptic
None.

No. 08Pointer

Shape shifter

The pointer takes the Mac’s cursor shape, and its dots rearrange instead of blinking.

Where it goes
Live session, driven by Mac cursor telemetry: arrow, I-beam, pointing hand.
Build it in SwiftUI
Quick path: SF Symbols cursorarrow, character.cursor.ibeam, hand.point.up.left with .contentTransition(.symbolEffect(.replace)). Dot path: an animatable dot array in Canvas on .snappy(duration: 0.18).
Haptic
None. It changes shape constantly, and buzzing would get old in a minute.

No. 09Pointer

The shy pointer

An easter egg. The pointer dodges your finger twice, then gives in.

Where it goes
Home art only, when someone pokes the illustration. Never in a real session.
Build it in SwiftUI
DragGesture(minimumDistance: 0) reports finger distance; the pointer’s .offset moves on .spring(duration: 0.35, bounce: 0.45); a dodges counter ends the game.
Haptic
.impact(flexibility: .soft, intensity: 0.4) per dodge, .success when it gives in.

No. 10Feedback

Click ripple, three ways

Three ways a click can land on dots. Tap anywhere, then switch the style.

Where it goes
Coach pad and Home art. In a session the ring is an overlay above the crisp picture, never a filter on it.
Build it in SwiftUI
Ring: a Canvas circle from .keyframeAnimator(initialValue: 0, trigger: clicks). Wave: a WWDC24-style ripple, .layerEffect(ShaderLibrary.ripple(.float2(origin), .float(elapsed)), maxSampleOffset: CGSize(width: 12, height: 12)), on art only.
Haptic
.impact(weight: .heavy) on every click.

No. 11Feedback

Right-click double ring

Two fingers, two rings. The second ring lands 70 ms late, so it reads as the other click.

Where it goes
Live session secondary click. The Mac’s own context menu follows.
Build it in SwiftUI
A two-touch UITapGestureRecognizer bridged with UIGestureRecognizerRepresentable (iOS 18); rings from .phaseAnimator.
Haptic
Two rigid transients 70 ms apart (CHHapticPattern), lighter than a click.

No. 12Feedback

Lift and drag

Hold still and the contact dot fills a ring. When it’s full, whatever sits under the pointer lifts.

Where it goes
Live session: hold to drag files, select text, move windows.
Build it in SwiftUI
LongPressGesture(minimumDuration: 0.4).sequenced(before: DragGesture()) with @GestureState; the lift is .scaleEffect plus .shadow on .spring(duration: 0.3, bounce: 0.3).
Haptic
.impact(weight: .medium) at lift, .impact(weight: .light) on drop.

No. 13Feedback

Gesture coach

Three short lessons on a practice pad. Nothing reaches your Mac, so it’s safe to be bad at it.

Where it goes
First run, and “How to steer · 40 sec” on Home. The real coach has five lessons: move, click, scroll, drag, zoom.
Build it in SwiftUI
A lesson enum drives .phaseAnimator ghost fingers; success copy swaps with .transition(.blurReplace); MagnifyGesture runs the zoom lesson.
Haptic
.selection per step, .impact(weight: .heavy) for the practice click.

No. 14Feedback

Dictation pours in

Your voice as a dot waveform. On Done, the dots pour across and become typing on the Mac.

Where it goes
Dock sheet, Mic button.
Build it in SwiftUI
A Canvas waveform fed by input levels from an AVAudioEngine tap; the typed text reveals glyph by glyph with a TextRenderer (iOS 18).
Haptic
.start when listening begins, .stop on Done.

No. 15Feedback

Clipboard crossing

A small packet of dots carries what you copied across the gap, in whichever direction you sent it.

Where it goes
Dock, Clip. Copy from Mac after ⌘C; Paste to Mac through the system paste button.
Build it in SwiftUI
Packet position from KeyframeAnimator with CubicKeyframes along an arc; the toast uses .transition(.move(edge: .bottom).combined(with: .opacity)); PasteButton handles Paste to Mac.
Haptic
.impact(weight: .light) when the packet lands.

No. 16States

Reconnecting, in place

We keep your view where it was and dim it through a dot screen. Taps made while we’re away are dropped, not replayed.

Where it goes
Live session, during short network drops. Back within 15 minutes means no re-pairing.
Build it in SwiftUI
Dim with .colorEffect(ShaderLibrary.dotScreen(.float(amount))); the pill text uses .contentTransition(.numericText()); the marching dots are .symbolEffect(.variableColor.iterative) on ellipsis.
Haptic
None while retrying. .impact(weight: .light) when it’s back.

No. 17States

Your Mac is napping

An Atkinson-dithered nap with floating z’s. Try again knocks; the Mac may or may not care.

Where it goes
The friendly error when the Mac sleeps or its lid is closed. One fix, one button.
Build it in SwiftUI
Render the art once into a Canvas; the z’s ride KeyframeAnimator offsets under the same Bayer .layerEffect; each knock is a .phaseAnimator ring.
Haptic
.warning when a knock gets no answer.

No. 18States

Anywhere, gently

Away from home, the hand stops at the edge of your Wi-Fi. One calm offer and an easy no.

Where it goes
Tapping Connect while away from home on the free plan.
Build it in SwiftUI
The offer is a .sheet with .presentationDetents([.height(220)]); the Wi-Fi edge is a dotted Circle().stroke(style: StrokeStyle(lineWidth: 2, dash: [1, 6])) that ripples with .phaseAnimator.
Haptic
None for the offer. We don’t buzz people to sell them things. .success only if they connect.

No. 19States

Agent needs you

When an agent on your Mac is waiting on you, the live dot turns into a beacon.

Where it goes
Home Mac card, the push notification, and the session edge. Ships as a beta in 1.0.
Build it in SwiftUI
.phaseAnimator([false, true]) with no trigger loops two rings for as long as the view is up; Reduce Motion shows the two rings still.
Haptic
One .impact(weight: .light) when it appears in the app. Pushes use the system alert.

No. 20System

Dynamic Island takeover

Compact says someone needs you. Expanded says who and why. One more tap and you’re in.

Where it goes
The Live Activity for a running session or a waiting agent.
Build it in SwiftUI
ActivityKit: DynamicIsland { DynamicIslandExpandedRegion(.bottom) { Button(intent: OpenSession()) } } compactLeading: { Mark() } compactTrailing: { EmberDot() } minimal: { EmberDot() }; the takeover is .widgetURL into a .navigationTransition(.zoom).
Haptic
The system alert from AlertConfiguration on the update. Nothing extra.

No. 21System

Live Activity in dots

An agent’s progress as a row of dots that fill one update at a time. The last dot turns ember when it needs you.

Where it goes
Lock Screen and StandBy during a session or an agent run.
Build it in SwiftUI
Live Activities only animate between updates: an HStack of Circle()s keyed to context.state.done, .contentTransition(.numericText()), and Text(timerInterval:countsDown:) for the clock.
Haptic
None, except the system alert when it needs you.

No. 22System

Menu bar: live tip, power down

The mark’s tip glows ember while a phone is connected. Stop Sharing powers down like an old TV.

Where it goes
The Mac menu bar icon and its popover.
Build it in SwiftUI
A MenuBarExtra label is an image, so swap pre-rendered NSImage frames (template mark, ember tip) on a slow timer. The popover strip powers down with KeyframeAnimator on scaleY, then scaleX, then opacity.
Haptic
None on the Mac. The phone gets .warning with “Stopped from your Mac”.

No. 23System

Splash from dust

Dust gathers into the mark, then the name. The tip lights last.

Where it goes
First launch only. After that the app opens straight to Home.
Build it in SwiftUI
The launch screen (UILaunchScreen) can’t animate, so the first view starts on that exact frame and runs a KeyframeAnimator over dot offsets in a Canvas; gate it with @AppStorage("didLaunch").
Haptic
None. Nobody wants a buzz on launch.

No. 24System

Dock sheet through dots

Pull the handle and the desktop dims through a dot screen instead of a blur. The sheet lands on a spring.

Where it goes
Live session. Swipe up for Keys, Mic, Clip, Fit and Mode.
Build it in SwiftUI
.presentationDetents([.height(96), .medium]) with .presentationBackgroundInteraction(.enabled(upThrough: .height(96))); the dim is a .colorEffect dot-screen shader whose radius follows the sheet height.
Haptic
.impact(weight: .light) when it snaps to a detent.

Notes

Built on the Reach system in FARSIDE-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md: void, bone, ash and one ember; Doto only for big numbers; eases (.16, 1, .3, 1) and (.22, 1, .36, 1). These are web sketches of the feel. Tune durations and springs on a real iPhone with haptics on.

Nothing on this page talks to a Mac. It only looks like it does.
