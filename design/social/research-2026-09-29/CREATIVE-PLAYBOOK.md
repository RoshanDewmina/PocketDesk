# Farside social creative playbook (built on SOCIAL-RESEARCH.md)

Researched 29 Sep 2026. I only read; nothing was posted, signed up for or contacted. I read `/Users/roshansilva/Developer/PocketDesk/design/social/SOCIAL-RESEARCH.md` first and don't repeat it here, so there's nothing on ranking signals, safe zones, hashtags or cadence. The labels are the same as in that doc: **[P]** the platform or another primary source, **[T]** trade press, **[V]** a vendor or practitioner (take it as a direction, not a rule), **[C]** a founder's or agency's own account, **[I]** my inference or opinion. Every hook follows the existing copy rules: no latency or user numbers, no prices, "Anywhere" is always "coming", "agent needs you" is always "beta", and never "the far side".

---

## 0. The short version

1. **Each post proves one verb in one shot.** The thumb moves and the pointer moves in the same frame. That shot is Farside's moat: it's hard to fake, and the competitors lead with agents, not touch. [I]
2. **Keep two layers and never mix them.** The dithered brand layer covers backgrounds, silhouettes and type. The proof layer is real UI and real footage, always crisp. [I]
3. **Open on the problem moment in the viewer's words:** "other room", "Mac mini with no monitor", "agent waiting for a yes". Features come after. [I]
4. **The category got crowded in 2026.** Astropad Workbench launched 8 Apr 2026 as a remote desktop "for the AI era", not for IT support [T]. Claude Code Remote Control is pitched as "pick it up from your phone while you take a walk" [P]. Google's Antigravity Remote Control followed on 21 Aug 2026 [V]. Farside should win on touch and the whole Mac ("any app, under your thumb"), not on "chat with your agent". [I]
5. **Never generate the product, the hands or the UI** (see §5).

## 1. Formats per platform

App-rec series: @bramdmedia's "turn your iPhone into a control panel for your Mac" got 6.5k likes [V] | Hand-on-phone POV with native text overlays; photo-mode "things I do from bed now" | 9–20 s | "Did you know…", "POV:", "Things I do from bed now:" | A large hand and phone, with the Mac readable behind | 4–5 a week |
| **YouTube Shorts** | Like TikTok, but searchable and evergreen. Google's own Shorts guidance is to feel like creator content, casual and speaking straight to the viewer [P]. Every start or replay has counted as a view since 31 Mar 2025 [P]. Shorts can run up to 3 min [V] | Title the Short like a search query: "Use your iPhone as a Mac trackpad" | 15–35 s | Answer first ("Here's how to control your Mac from your iPhone.") | Clear tutorial over vibe; link a 2-min walkthrough as the related video | 3 a week (uploaded natively) |

**How the platforms differ [I]:**
- **Tone.** X is dry and technical, with receipts. Threads is warmer and led by questions. TikTok and Reels feel like a person in a room. Shorts should read as the answer to a search.
- **Text density.** Reels and TikTok: 7 words or fewer on screen at a time. X can carry a whole sentence. Carousels: 25 words or fewer per slide.
- **Native features:**
  - TikTok: native text overlays, plus its text-to-speech voice used sparingly (it reads as native).
  - Instagram: Trial Reels once the account nears ~1,000 followers, for A/B testing two hooks on the same footage [V].
  - Threads: Snippets for build logs.
  - X: captions must be burned in, because X's own captions are off by default [V].

## 3. The "code-y" technical aesthetic

**What the evidence shows [V]:**
- Lo-fi pixel art and dither appear on the 2026 trend lists (Kittl, The Branding Journal).
- Dither and ASCII effects are now drop-in components (Paper Shaders, Aceternity UI, Framer). A look you can install as a component is a look that has become common.
- Kittl's 2026 list also names "Blueprint" (spec labels, exploded views), "Surveillance" (security-camera UI) and gritty styles as the reaction against smooth AI imagery.
- Granola went the opposite way, with handwriting, and was rewarded for it.

**What still reads technical and premium [I]:**
- Blueprint callouts with thin leader lines and real values ("clipboard: both ways", "pointer: always on top").
- Status lines in monospace with timestamps ("11:48 PM · reached · studio-mac").
- One dot-matrix word or number per frame, never a paragraph of it.
- Halftone used as a *silhouette mask* (hand, pointer, Mac) on near-black.
- Real, redacted text from real agent sessions.

**What's now a cliché [I, opinion]:**
- Screen Studio's default look.
- Purple-blue "AI glow" orbs.
- Matrix rain.
- RGB glitch.
- CRT scanlines over the whole frame.
- A typewriter tagline with a blinking block cursor.
- An ASCII hero image that dissolves into the logo.
- Lo-fi beats over desk b-roll.
- Fake code.

**One Farside-specific warning [I]:** the product literally shows your screen remotely, so security-camera styling (grid overlays, REC dots) reads as creepy. Aim for "yours", not "being watched".

## 4. Making the use clear in 1–2 s

**Framing patterns:**
1. **Cause and effect in one frame.** Thumb slides, pointer slides, with no cut between them; a cut is what makes people suspect fakery. [I]
2. **Problem, payoff, second beat, loop.**
   - Problem, 1 s at most: the Mac across the room, the waiting agent prompt, an "Allow" dialog.
   - Payoff: the pointer moves.
   - Second beat: the click lands with a tap ring.
   - This mirrors Linear's start → action → result [V] and TikTok's advice to state the proposition in the first 3 s [P].
3. **On-screen text formulas** (7 words or fewer at frame 0, and the verb visible within 1 s):
   - Identity swap: "Your iPhone is now your Mac's trackpad."
   - Location split: "You: couch. Mac: desk."
   - Inversion: "iPhone Mirroring, but backwards."
   - Tip: "Did you know your iPhone can…"
   - Status line: "Your Mac is napping."

**How to show a phone controlling a Mac convincingly:**

*Shots:*
- **Hero proof shot.** A three-quarter angle over the shoulder: the phone in hand, sharp in the foreground, with the Mac screen readable behind.
  - Film it on a phone camera, which keeps both devices in focus. A shallow-focus DSLR will blur one of them.
- **Top-down desk shot.** Phone and MacBook side by side, the hand entering from the bottom. This is the clearest shot for tutorials.
- **9:16 split screen.**
  - Top: the Mac capture, cropped 2–3× around the pointer.
  - Bottom: the phone capture in a thin device frame.
  - An ember line joins the touch point to the pointer.
  - To sync the two recordings, use a click that visibly flashes on both. [I]
- **Picture-in-picture.** The Mac capture full-bleed, with real handheld phone footage in a corner inside the safe zone. It proves it's real and keeps the UI crisp.
- **"One take" badge.** Open with 2–3 s of continuous camera footage labeled "one take", then switch to the crisp screen captures. [I]

*Filming and editing:*
- **Filming screens.**
  - Set the Mac to a fixed 60 Hz and match the camera shutter (1/60), which stops flicker.
  - Turn off True Tone and auto-brightness.
  - Change the angle or distance to get rid of moiré.
  - Expose for the screens. [V]
- **Haptics don't show up on camera.** Add a ring pulse at the fingertip plus click foley. [I/V]
- **Zoom.** The UI element that matters should fill at least a third of the frame width. A whole Mac desktop squeezed into 1080 px wide is unreadable. [I]
- **App Store previews.** They must be screen-only (no hands, no people), 30 s at most, up to 3 per language [P]. Record clean device captures on every shoot so they double as App Store previews.

## 5. AI-generated footage

**Backlash data:**
- Coca-Cola's 2025 AI Christmas ad: positive sentiment fell from 23.8% to 10.2% [T].
- McDonald's Netherlands pulled its AI Christmas ad within about three days (Dec 2025) [T].
- IAB survey, Jan 2026 (505 Gen Z and Millennial consumers):
  - 45% feel positive about AI ads, while 82% of ad executives assume they do.
  - 39% of Gen Z feel negative.
  - 73% say disclosing AI use would raise their purchase likelihood or leave it unchanged. [P, industry body]
- NIM study: identical ads were rated less natural and less useful when labeled as AI, but AI is tolerated more for high-tech products [V].
- Gartner (via Grey Journal): 50% of US consumers prefer brands that don't use generative AI in customer-facing content [V].

**Where it's accepted:** Kalshi's NBA Finals ad, made with Google's Veo 3, cost about $2,000, took two days and drew 3M+ views on X in a week. It worked because it was openly absurd and made no product claim [T].

**Farside's rule [I]:**
- **Allowed**, and labeled on every platform: generated atmosphere plates behind real UI (an empty office at night, rain on a window, a Mac mini in a closet), abstract textures, obviously unreal jokes.
- **Never generated:** hands, phones, Macs, pointer motion, UI, or "customers". A remote-control app lives on "it really works", and one fake-looking hand undoes the proof shot.

**Labels:**
- TikTok, Meta and X rules are already in SOCIAL-RESEARCH.md.
- YouTube adds an "altered or synthetic content" toggle for realistic content only; clearly unrealistic or animated work doesn't need it [P].

## 6. Creators and UGC

**Evidence:**
- Wispr Flow's creator program is summarized in §2.
- Kalshi: one small creator got 50M views on a single video, while creators with 1M+ followers got "a few million" [V].
- Typical costs:
  - UGC video: $75–300 [V]. DansUGC lists $32 for a reaction clip, $97 for a talking head and $292 for full UGC [V].
  - Mid-tier creator retainers: $4–8k a month [V].
  - Dev YouTube sponsorships: no rate card you can defend from public data [V].

**Verdict: worth doing, but small. [I]**
- **Who:** 6–10 creators in two groups:
  - Mac and iOS tips and app-recommendation creators (numbered "apps I use" series).
  - AI-coding creators. Coding from your phone is already a genre: Riley Brown builds apps "from his phone on a couch" [V].
- **Budget:** about $1,500–3,000 in total.
  - $100–250 base per video, 1–2 videos per creator.
  - A bonus, e.g. $250 at 250k views.
  - Lifetime access to the paid plan.
- **Timing:** recruit now. Give creators TestFlight access about 3 weeks before 17 Nov, and have posts go live launch week.
- **One-page brief:**
  - The promise line.
  - Three must-shows: thumb and pointer in the same frame; voice typing into a Mac text field; one deadpan status line.
  - Five hooks from §8.
  - Full creative freedom on half the posts (Wispr's method).
  - Don'ts:
    - No latency or speed numbers.
    - "Anywhere" is "coming"; agent alerts are "beta".
    - No private information on screen.
    - No AI-generated product footage.
  - Deliver a clean master without captions, plus raw footage, with 90-day usage rights.
- **Disclosure:**
  - TikTok: creators must switch on the commercial content toggle. Undisclosed branded posts can lose For You eligibility; enforcement was set to cover all US accounts by March 2026 [V].
  - Instagram: the Paid partnership label.
  - Every paid post also needs a plain "ad" in the caption, because the FTC doesn't accept platform labels on their own [V].
- **Replicate winners:** if a creator's post beats their usual numbers about 3× on day one, turn it into the next brief for everyone.

## 9. The code-y aesthetic: do and don't

**Do:**
- Keep the UI and screen captures native-sharp, and dither only the brand layer.
- Use dither cells at least 4–6 px wide on a 1080 canvas, monochrome, and mostly in mid-tones. Platforms re-encode fine noise into mush [V].
- Test before posting: re-encode at about 10–15 Mbps, then watch it on a phone at arm's length.
- Use mono only for labels and status lines, and a sans for sentences. Make hook text roughly 60 px tall or more on 1080×1920. [I]
- Use ember only for the point of contact: the pointer tip, the tap ring, the one key word.
- Use one texture per frame.
- Use real, redacted terminal text.

**Don't:**
- Dither the UI, or use 1-px Bayer dither patterns.
- Use CRT effects over the full frame, glitch, or Matrix rain.
- Use Screen Studio's default gradient look.
- Show fake code.
- Use security-camera grids or REC dots.
- Use AI-generated hands or devices.
- Show more than 7 words at frame 0.
